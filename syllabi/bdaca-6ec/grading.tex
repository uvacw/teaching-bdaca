The final grade for this course is composed of an individual in-class coding exercise (25\%), an individual project pitch (10\%), and a final individual project (65\%).

\subsection*{In-class coding exercise (25\%)}

The Week 4 in-class coding exercise assesses students' individual ability to work with Python-based data-analysis workflows and their understanding of the fundamental concepts introduced during the first part of the course. Students are asked to read, adapt, test, debug, and/or explain code using fundamental techniques taught in the course. The exercise also includes short questions about relevant course literature. The exercise is completed individually and without the use of generative-AI tools, subject to the applicable examination arrangements.

Grading criteria are communicated to students together with the assignment. In general, the assessment considers:

\begin{itemize}
    \item students' understanding of Python basics and fundamental course concepts;
    \item their ability to work independently with basic data-analysis code;
    \item the correctness and clarity of their code, explanations, and interpretations; and
    \item the quality of their responses to questions related to relevant course literature and methods.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Individual project pitch (10\%)}

In Week 7, students give an individual pitch of their proposed final project. The pitch assesses the feasibility and methodological justification of the proposed research plan. It also assesses students' ability to justify methodological choices, acknowledge a relevant limitation or risk in the planned research, and respond constructively to critical questions.

Grading criteria are communicated to students together with the assignment. In general, the assessment considers:

\begin{itemize}
    \item the clarity and feasibility of the proposed research project;
    \item the justification of the proposed data, research design, and analytical strategy; and
    \item the student's critical reflection on limitations or risks and ability to answer questions about the proposed work.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Final individual project (65\%)}

The final individual project is an individual computational communication-science research project. It accounts for 65\% of the final grade and assesses students' ability to formulate and answer a research question using fundamental techniques taught in the course.

Detailed requirements concerning the report, project files, critical reflection, submission, attribution, and permitted generative-AI use are provided in Section~\ref{sec:final-project}.

\subsection*{Grading and second attempt}

Students must receive a passing grade (5.5 or higher) for each of the three individual assessment components: the in-class coding exercise, the individual project pitch, and the final individual project. If the grade for one component is lower than 5.5, the student may complete one reassessment opportunity for that component, in the form and by the deadline communicated by the lecturer.

A reassessment of the in-class coding exercise is completed individually in a supervised setting and without generative-AI tools. A reassessment of the project pitch takes the form of an individual pitch or oral assessment. An improved version of the final individual project may not be graded higher than 6.0. If a reassessment is graded lower than 5.5, the course cannot be completed.
